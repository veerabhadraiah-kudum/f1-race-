## Why these choices (editorial guardrails)

- **Punch at the performance, not the person.** Every joke is about public
  rhetoric, campaign imagery and political theatre — Vision dates, padayatra
  imagery, Twitter activity, "rowdy" branding. No private life, no family, no
  criminal allegation, no caste, no language/region slur.
- **Bipartisan aim.** Scene 3 says "change the party, the dialogue stays", which
  is the whole joke: the same playbook shows up everywhere. That keeps it
  comedy rather than a party ad.
- **The voter is the hero.** The last beat hands the punchline to the electorate,
  not to a rival politician, so the video does not read as campaign material.
- **The disclaimer is inside the video**, not just in the description — it is on
  the title card, the HUD chip and the end card.
- **No named accusation is voiced.** "CBN" and "Lokesh" appear as character
  labels in the title card; the jokes themselves are about public behaviour.

## If you want to change the tone

- **Softer:** cut the "చెప్పులు" (slipper) line and end on "ఓటే అసలైన రౌడీ".
- **Harder:** keep the visuals but add a receipt-based punchline — jokes about
  promises made vs delivered are stronger than personal jibes and safer legally.
- **Longer:** the timeline is generated from the VO lengths, so add a fifth
  `dict(kind="scene", ...)` entry and a matching take.
